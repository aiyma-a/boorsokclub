ingredients:
  - 500 g plain flour, plus extra for dusting
  - 250 ml warm milk
  - 7 g (1 packet) instant dried yeast
  - 1 tbsp sugar
  - 1 tsp salt
  - 1 egg
  - 30 g butter, melted
  - Neutral oil for deep frying (about 1 litre)
steps:
  - Stir the yeast and sugar into the warm milk and leave it for 10 minutes, until it turns foamy.
  - In a large bowl, mix the flour and salt. Add the yeast milk, the egg and the melted butter, then bring everything together into a soft dough.
  - Knead for 8–10 minutes until smooth and slightly springy. Cover and leave somewhere warm for about an hour, until doubled in size.
  - Roll the dough out on a floured surface to about 1 cm thick. Cut it into 3 cm squares or diamonds.
  - Heat the oil in a deep pan to 170–175 °C. A scrap of dough should rise and sizzle straight away.
  - Fry in small batches for 1–2 minutes per side, turning once, until puffed and deep golden.
  - Lift out onto paper towels. Serve warm with tea, butter, honey or jam.

In Kyrgyzstan, boorsok is how a table says *welcome*. The little squares get piled high on the dastorkon at weddings, holidays and memorial gatherings, and any time guests drop by for tea.

## Why you'll love it

- **Five pantry staples.** Flour, milk, yeast, egg and butter.
- **Make-ahead friendly.** Boorsok keeps for days in a sealed tin.
- **Endlessly snackable.** Pair it with tea, butter, honey or jam, or eat it straight from the bowl.

## Tips for the puffiest boorsok

Keep the oil steady. Too cool and the dough soaks up oil; too hot and the outside browns before the middle cooks. Fry a test piece first and adjust.
